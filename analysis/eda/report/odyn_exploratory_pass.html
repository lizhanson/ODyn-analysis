What the ODyn data can actually support

A full pass over 44 sessions of bulbar dopaminergic imaging — coverage, signed responses, population geometry, and arousal coupling — run to decide what is worth reporting. The conclusions here are now implemented in the figure code.

44 usable sessions of 48 404,415 unit–odor rows 6 cohorts · 13 mice 16-odor panel · pre/post ket-xyl run 2026-08-30
SUM

What is worth reporting

Ranked by how well the design and the data support the claim, not by how interesting it sounds.

Strong

Anesthesia abolishes suppression, everywhere

Suppression breadth collapses from 0.08–0.48 awake to 0.00–0.07 under ket/xyl in every cohort and every compartment. The largest and most consistent effect in the dataset, and a within-session, within-field contrast — the strongest design available here.

Strong

A depth gradient in excitation/suppression balance

Awake somatic E/S balance orders monotonically: DAT superficial +0.67, TH superficial +0.34, TH deep −0.41. TH deep is the only suppression-dominated population, and the contrast with TH superficial is within-mouse in all four TH animals.

Strong

Arousal amplifies odor responses rather than suppressing activity

Trial-to-trial running deviation predicts larger responses in every cohort, and pupil dilation does the same about three times more weakly. Both act on excitation and leave suppression uncoupled. Coupling is zero across the whole pre-odor window, so it is not motion artifact. This is the opposite sign to the NE-suppression hypothesis as posed.

Strong

The signed 4-second mean hides most of the response structure

Of unit–odor pairs that are genuinely bidirectional within the odor window, the signed mean misses 69–88% awake. Across all pairs the trace method finds a response in 54–96% where the mean-based call finds one in 12–66%.

Strong

Glomerular signals carry far more odor information than DA somata

Sixteen-way classification runs at 78–93% at 10× against 27–59% at 20×, despite comparable unit counts. Expected, since 20× samples a small subset of spatially distributed labeled-line input channels — but it sets the scale for everything below.

Qualified

Ratio discrimination is carried by one mixture pair, and it emerges late

Only pair 39/40 separates above chance (27.6% of tests at p<0.05 against 5% expected). It builds through the odor and peaks in the final second. Pair 17/18 is at most marginally above chance anywhere. Mixture work is parked until there is more data.

Qualified

10× session variability is acquisition, not sampling

At 10× the field of view is the whole dorsal surface and does not differ between sessions, so sampling cannot explain why sessions differ. What does track it is acquisition date, and the effect survives within line. Odor batch is the leading candidate; that is covered in the separate acquisition-QC report.

Not supported

Spontaneous arousal events outside odor epochs

Only 1 of 37 awake sessions has enough clean quiet→run transitions in odor-free windows. The auxiliary product stores data per trial, fragmenting the record; recovering this needs continuous inter-trial data.

The animal-level structure decides which comparisons can carry weight, and it inverts the intuitive ordering: the depth and anesthesia contrasts are paired designs, while the headline line comparison rests on two animals.

01

Three join traps

Verified across all 38 sessions holding both an imaging and an auxiliary product. Each would corrupt the arousal analysis silently rather than raising an error.

Trap 1 · universal

State codes are inverted between products. Grouped files order state_levels as ('post','pre'); auxiliary files as ('pre','post'). Joining on the integer state swaps awake and anesthetized in every session.

Trap 2

20× grouped trial_id is a positional arange, not the database id. 10× files do carry real ids, so no universal key exists in the grouped products.

Trap 3

Grouped products drop trials the auxiliary still holds. Lengths differ in 23 of 38 sessions and the offset is nonzero in 17, up to 24 trials. A positional join is correct in only 21 of 38 sessions.

Resolved. The source extraction round carries database trial ids, its order matches the grouped product one-for-one, and its ids are all present in the auxiliary — verified on sessions with offsets of 2, 4, 21 and 24. population_metrics.load_population uses them, and states resolve through each file's own levels.

What the four-second mean hides

Excitation and suppression are now integrated separately over the waveform, so a response that is excited and then suppressed is not averaged away.

Why responder calls reference the pre-odor baseline

Mineral oil is not a clean null in this preparation, and how far it departs from one varies by scale, by session and by odor batch. Detection therefore references each unit's own pre-odor excursions rather than the blank, so the cutoff cannot drift with a consumable. The size and behaviour of the blank response are an acquisition-QC matter and are treated separately.

04

Population geometry

Crossvalidated distances are unbiased but noisy at these trial counts and hard to read across sixteen odors. Two cheaper views are kept beside them, because legibility is what generates hypotheses — provided nothing is claimed from the biased ones.

How much odor information each population carries

The 10×/20× gap is expected: 20× samples a small subset of labeled-line input channels that are distributed across the dorsal surface, so it sees fewer of them. What the classifier adds is that every population is well above chance — TH deep somata still classify 16 odors at four times chance — and that Thy1 is the most informative population in the dataset, which supports using it as the 10× anchor.

What gets mistaken for what

The structure is specific: mixtures are confused with their reciprocal partner, never with their components. In Thy1, lambda is never mistaken for acetophenone or limonene. Ratio is the only failure mode, and it fails for two of the three pairs.

The trial-by-trial view is the most hypothesis-generating of the three, and it says the mixture families are not alike. The alpha mixture is a novel pattern relative to its components; the epsilon mixture is dominated by them; lambda sits between and is the one pair whose ratios separate. Whether ratios are discriminable may therefore be downstream of how a mixture relates to its components — testable without more data.

Where the session variability comes from

At 10× the field of view is the whole dorsal surface and does not differ between sessions, so sampling cannot explain why sessions differ. What does correlate is acquisition date, and it survives within line (pooled within-line ρ = +0.48, p = 0.0012; DAT alone ρ = +0.76). Every session through 2026-07-17 has 191–224 trials and every session from 2026-08-03 has 159–160, with the later ones discriminating better despite fewer repeats — so it is not a trial-count artifact. Something changed around Aug 3 that is worth identifying, because it is the largest single source of 10× session variance.

05

Arousal coupling

Awake trials only — locomotion is identically zero under ket/xyl, a useful check on the state labels. Odor identity is removed from both sides before correlating, because odor delivery itself drives running: the fraction of awake samples above 1 cm/s rises from 0.108 pre-odor to 0.205 during the odor, consistently across all six cohorts.

Coupling is flat at zero across the entire pre-odor period, rises 1 s after onset and peaks at 2 s. Running occurs in the baseline window too, so a motion or hemodynamic artifact would appear there; it does not. Baseline coupling is −0.002 against +0.190 in the odor window, and only 4 of 57 session–population cases have baseline ≥ odor (p < 0.0001). The signature is gain modulation of the odor response, not a drive on activity.

Separating running from pupil · paired across 44 session–populations

Pupil controlling for running: +0.055 (p = 0.0025). Running controlling for pupil: +0.189 (p < 0.0001). Both survive, with running roughly three times larger. The exception is TH 20× superficial somata, the one population where pupil is the better predictor (+0.136 against running's +0.034). Pupil is also asymmetric: dilation trials give +0.03 to +0.17 while constriction trials sit at zero or below, so the relationship is carried by dilations rather than being a symmetric axis.

The locomotion-free control, and a data-quality problem

Under ket/xyl running is identically zero while pupil still varies, and pupil quality is at its best there. If pupil indexed a movement-independent state signal, this is where it would show — it does not (−0.11 to +0.09, no time structure). Separately, awake pupil coverage is lost mainly to clipping, up to 41% of awake samples, and clipping happens when the pupil is large. The unmasked trace with blinks removed is primary here; repeating everything on the quality-masked trace gives the same conclusions, slightly attenuated. The awake pupil ROI or fit bounds are worth fixing before pupil carries weight in a figure.

06

What changed in the code

The 10× and 20× paths now share one implementation, so the scales are measured identically rather than through differently defined quantities.

  • Responder calls reference pre-odor excursions, stratified by trial count, replacing the earlier blank-referenced cutoff. The old path is retained but marked superseded, since earlier tables came from it.
  • Excitation and suppression are integrated separately over the waveform, with onset/early/late windows, peak latencies and an early–late index. Biphasic responses are detectable for the first time.
  • Lifetime sparseness now comes from continuous AUC rather than thresholded excess, which removes a bias: previously, non-responsive units returned NaN and dropped out, so the metric was conditioned on responsiveness in a state-dependent way.
  • Whole-panel confusion matrices and RDMs are generated alongside the pairwise geometry, and both runners emit them.
  • Joins use the source round's database trial ids, and states resolve through each file's own levels.
  • One crossnobis implementation, with a permutation null that reports its resolution and flags underpowered rather than emitting a z score.
  • Still open: lagged and FIR models of the arousal coupling; respiration-phase regressors; the z-stack soma-size×depth model; and the Aug 3 acquisition change.
MTH

How these numbers were made

  • Detection. Trials reduce to a median waveform per unit and odor, box-smoothed over 0.5 s so a minimum duration is enforced without a separate persistence rule. Thresholds are quantiles of the maximal pre-odor excursion of those same waveforms — matched unit, trials, smoothing and window length — and stratified by trial count, since a median over more trials is quieter. Calibration was checked by running the identical procedure on two windows inside the pre-odor period: the realised rate is 0.035–0.078 against a nominal 0.05.
  • Distances are split-half crossvalidated and noise-normalised, so their expectation is zero without a reliable difference and negative values are expected on noise. Nulls are empirical permutation p values reported with their resolution; a null at its floor is labelled underpowered.
  • Sampling hierarchy. Units to session medians to mouse means to cohort. Between-line contrasts are descriptive throughout, because DAT and Thy1 rest on two animals; inferential weight sits on the within-mouse depth contrast and the within-session anesthesia contrast.
  • Heavy tails. Ratio distances are strongly outlier-driven — two TH superficial sessions give 39/40 values of 12.4 and 4.3 while the rest sit near zero — so medians are used at every level, and mean-based aggregation of these quantities is misleading.
  • Reproducing this. Extraction reads each session once and caches a unit × trial × 0.5 s array locally; every analysis above runs off that cache. The stage scripts are in analysis/eda/.
