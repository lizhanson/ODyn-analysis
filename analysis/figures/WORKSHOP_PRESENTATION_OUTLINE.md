# OB dopamine workshop presentation

## Communication job

By the end, experts working on olfactory-bulb dopamine neurons should see that
TH- and DAT-based access strategies sample functionally heterogeneous
populations; that depth, compartment, and brain state expose additional
organization; and that this imaging platform can connect that diversity to
odor-map structure and principal-neuron output without claiming that the line
comparison alone identifies a mechanism.

The fixed comparative spine is:

1. TH versus DAT at 10x glomerular scale.
2. Superficial TH versus DAT at 20x cellular scale.
3. Superficial versus deep TH at 20x.

## Proposed 14-slide core, with one optional arousal slide

### 1. Dopamine-neuron diversity across scale and state

**Job:** Establish the topic and the lab's niche: odor-response flexibility is
the main program; the direct TH/DAT comparison is a focused workshop project.

**Visual:** One strong 10x field and one 20x field; minimal title copy.

### 2. TH and DAT access overlapping, non-equivalent populations

**Claim:** Genetic access strategy is part of the biological definition of the
recorded population.

**Visual:** Compact literature framing from Grubb/Galliano beside a simple
marker/depth comparison. Avoid presenting TH and DAT as exhaustive subtypes.

### 3. Our tissue makes the line difference concrete

**Claim:** TH-GCaMP and DAT-GCaMP differ in depth, gross morphology, and TH/CR
colocalization; DAT-GCaMP positivity is not synonymous with TH positivity.

**Visual:** Best matched immunostaining fields plus quantified colocalization.

**Evidence gate:** Required. This is the direct identity evidence; mask area is
supporting morphology, not molecular identity.

### 4. One platform samples glomerular maps, cells, processes, and state

**Claim:** Paired 10x and 20x imaging connects population-scale odor maps to
cellular and compartment-resolved dynamics.

**Visual:** 10x/20x example images, awake-to-ket/xyl timeline, 16-odor panel
organized into three mixture families. Mention pupil, respiration, and
treadmill without spending a panel on them yet.

### 5. Dopaminergic odor maps are structured and line-dependent at 10x

**Claim:** TH and DAT fields contain reproducible odor-specific signed spatial
structure that is not adequately summarized by a field mean.

**Visual:** Matched-format 16-odor pages for DAT and TH, with one Thy1 page as a
principal-neuron reference. Add split-half map reliability when available.

**Caveat:** Cross-line spatial correspondence across animals requires dorsal
field registration; examples alone do not establish canonical line maps.

### 6. DAT is more selectively modulated across odors, but the difference is metric-dependent

**Claim:** DAT shows greater analog lifetime sparseness than TH at both scales;
at 20x this does not simply mean fewer odors cross a responder threshold.

**Visual:** Excitatory and suppressive lifetime sparseness beside signed
responder breadth, sessions nested within mice. Show 10x first, then 20x
superficial somas and soma-plus-process groups.

### 7. The superficial line comparison persists at cellular resolution

**Claim:** Superficial DAT and TH populations differ in odor modulation and
signed temporal structure even when compared at the same scale and depth.

**Visual:** Representative median ROI-by-odor heatmaps; adjacent soma and
grouped soma-plus-process summaries; reliability shown as a qualifier.

### 8. Depth reveals a suppression-rich TH population

**Claim:** Deep, large TH cells are less odor-specific and more
suppression-dominated than superficial TH cells.

**Visual:** Soma area distributions, depth/morphology examples, and paired
within-mouse signed response distributions. Soma and grouped-process panels are
adjacent.

**Language:** “Subtype-and-depth,” not subtype alone.

### 9. Mixture nonlinearity and reciprocal-mixture separation are different axes

**Claim:** The three mixture families engage different computations: departure
from the component-defined response axis need not predict the distance between
the reciprocal mixtures.

**Visual:** For all 17/18, 31/32, and 39/40, show component-axis residual beside
reciprocal-mixture difference-map magnitude in awake TH, DAT, and Thy1.

**Interpretation:** 17/18 is a candidate nonlinear family; 39/40 is a candidate
more-linear but more separable family. Do not cherry-pick either. This is the
segue from awake population diversity to the state manipulation.

**Current exploratory read:** This distinction is consistent across TH, DAT,
and Thy1 at 10x. Awake 17/18 has the largest residual from the empirical
component-response axis but the smallest reciprocal-mixture distance. Awake
39/40 lies closest to the component axis while showing the largest reciprocal
distance in DAT and Thy1.

### 10. Ket/xyl strongly lowers tonic fluorescence across DA populations

**Claim:** The most robust state effect is a widespread reduction in tonic F0
in TH and DAT populations.

**Visual:** Paired unit distributions summarized session-within-mouse for the
three fixed comparisons; show soma and group compartments at 20x.

### 11. Evoked responses compress and the suppressive tail largely disappears

**Claim:** Anesthesia changes signed response distributions, not merely their
mean amplitude.

**Visual:** Awake/post positive and negative AUC distributions, E/S balance,
and the low-tail change in Thy1 as a parallel observation.

**Interpretive gate:** Treat a lower-F0 floor as the leading explanation until
suppression survives F0/SNR matching and raw-dF/F sensitivity analyses.

### 12. Median population heatmaps expose the temporal state change

**Claim:** Awake responses contain heterogeneous, temporally structured
excitation and suppression. Anesthesia simplifies this signed structure, while
the amount of remaining excitation differs by line, depth, and compartment.

**Visual:** QC-style heatmaps of median trial responses for all ROIs and odors,
not selected single-cell traces. Use video only to make the same qualitative
point vivid.

### 13. Principal-neuron maps provide a parallel functional readout

**Claim:** Thy1 maps remain highly odor-informative, while pair-specific
mixture difference maps and a small suppressive tail change with state.

**Visual:** Thy1 reciprocal-mixture difference images before/after, followed by
the time-course question: when Thy1 maps diverge, do DA excitation,
suppression, or recruitment change at the same latency?

Pair the state comparison with the component-axis residual: it shrinks after
anesthesia, but some shrinkage can follow general response compression, so it
must remain adjacent to crossvalidated separation and the raw difference maps.

**Language:** Correspondence across separate mice, not causal coupling. Do not
claim a general loss of Thy1 coding or confusion under anesthesia.

**Current exploratory read:** There is no single DA recruitment feature that
tracks Thy1 separation across all families. Thy1 17/18 and 31/32 separation is
strongest early, whereas 31/32 DA separation emerges later. The clearest
temporal correspondence is 39/40, where Thy1 and DAT—but not TH—pattern
separation grow together. Treat that as a family-specific observation, not a
general DA mechanism.

### 14. Optional: state is both an experimental manipulation and an awake variable

**Claim:** Trial-to-trial awake arousal can alter tonic F0 or broaden the signed
response distribution, and respiration can distinguish active sampling from a
more general arousal association.

**Visual:** Include only if hierarchical results are coherent: detrended
pre-odor F0 versus pupil/running/respiration, plus high-versus-low arousal q10
and q90 response trajectories.

**Current exploratory read:** Running broadens the odor-period response
distribution mainly by increasing the positive tail; the effect is largest in
superficial TH and weakest in deep TH. It does not produce a general increase
in suppression. At baseline, DAT F0 covaries more consistently with respiration
frequency than with running, and the respiration association largely survives
partialling out running. These results favor active-sampling/gain language over
an arousal-driven suppression story. Pupil coverage limits the corresponding
pupil claim, and superficial TH auxiliary coverage is currently two mice. At
10x, DAT shows a particularly consistent positive shift during running—the
positive tail grows while the negative tail becomes less negative—whereas TH
is heterogeneous across mice.

### 15. Diversity requires context-rich nomenclature

**Synthesis:** Genetic marker, soma size/depth, compartment, imaging scale, and
brain state each change the population one describes. The shared opportunity
is to align nomenclature across those axes rather than treating TH and DAT as
interchangeable cell-type names.

**Visual:** A restrained synthesis using the three comparative anchors and the
capabilities this platform contributes. Close on concrete points for workshop
discussion and collaboration, not a generic “future directions” list.

## Minimum strong-data package

If the talk must be shortened, retain:

1. Immunostaining and line identity.
2. 10x TH/DAT spatial maps and specificity.
3. 20x superficial line comparison.
4. TH superficial/deep soma size and signed responses.
5. Tonic F0 state effect.
6. Loss of suppression plus the F0-floor interpretation.
7. One all-three-family mixture/Thy1 synthesis slide.

The temporal heatmaps, arousal result, and detailed mixture geometry are the
first items to move to backup if their across-mouse summaries are weak.
